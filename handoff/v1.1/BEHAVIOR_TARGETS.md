# Rival Behavior Targets — Initial Hypotheses

These notes capture the behaviors we care about. Do not convert them directly into hard-coded rules without evidence.

## Reference target: Nexto

### Keep / learn

- Fast conversion from loose ball to controlled possession.
- Strong dribble control.
- Rapid flick threat after possession.
- Effective ground offensive mechanics.
- Ability to remain dangerous without ideal boost.

### Investigate / improve

#### Fake-challenge susceptibility

Observed manual exploit:

1. Nexto has possession.
2. Opponent accelerates/boosts toward the ball as if committing.
3. Opponent aborts by braking, veering, or jumping without actually challenging.
4. Nexto may flick/release the ball early.
5. Opponent can collect the resulting possession.

Telemetry should eventually distinguish apparent challenge pressure from actual commitment using:

- distance
- closing velocity
- orientation
- acceleration/boost state if inferable
- jump/airborne state
- predicted time to contact
- whether the opponent's path remains ball-intersecting

Do not solve this in Milestone 01; make sure we will have data to solve it.

#### Boost indifference

Nexto can continue playing with very low boost and does not appear to place strong strategic value on boost acquisition.

We do not want to blindly copy that weakness.

## Reference target: Wisp v2-75B

### Keep / learn

- Strong map movement.
- Strong boost-denial behavior.
- Opponent starvation pressure.
- Advanced aerial capability.
- Possession/time-management tendencies when ahead.
- Strong overall decision quality.

### Investigate / improve

#### Resource-infeasible aerial commitments

Observed behavior:

- retreats or carries the ball toward own half
- attempts very long cross-map aerial/air-dribble offense
- may begin/continue the play with very low or exhausted boost
- may sacrifice possession or recovery quality

We eventually want a learned or well-founded concept of:

```text
available resources
+ reachable resources
- expected play cost
- recovery reserve
= resource margin
```

But do not impose a fixed boost threshold.

#### Boost greed / map-control tradeoff

Wisp's boost-starvation behavior is valuable, but it may overvalue boost routes relative to direct possession/pressure.

Future analysis should measure:

- possession lost after boost detours
- pressure lost after boost detours
- opponent starvation gained
- goals/shot value generated by the detour

## Strategic target

The desired bot is not simply "Wisp plus more boost" or "Nexto plus aerials."

The goal is a strong 1v1 policy that can eventually combine:

- Nexto-like ground possession/flick pressure
- Wisp-like boost denial and aerial mechanics
- better fake-challenge recognition
- better resource feasibility
- better score/clock-aware risk management
